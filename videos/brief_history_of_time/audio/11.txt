Yet the book's real subject is hidden in its title. Time, Hawking argues, is not simply a backdrop for events. It has a direction, and that direction needs explaining. A teacup slides off a table and shatters. Nothing in the laws of motion forbids the shards from leaping up and fusing back together, yet no one has ever seen it happen. Hawking ties this one-way arrow to the steady growth of disorder, to the way memory works, and to the expansion of the cosmos itself.
