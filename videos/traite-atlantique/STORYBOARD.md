# La traite atlantique: storyboard (draft for approval)

**Narration:** 14 clips, French. The film runs 5 min 22 s after 1.4 s of silence was added at
sentence ends. The voice says every number correctly; this was checked with a second
transcription: XVe, XIXe, 10 millions, 1400, 20 %.

**Style:** infinite canvas. One parchment board. A single pen draws one ChatGPT ink sketch per view
(lines only, no fill) and writes the words by hand. There are no cuts.

**Look:** history identity: parchment, brown ink, and oxblood as the only accent ink.

**Music:** an original bed composed in code, with two moods. The change comes at act 4, the
crossing.

**Text: more.** The pen writes places, dates, numbers, terms and a few key phrases the narration
stresses (for example « des biens », « une simple cargaison », « fondement racial »). Everything
written is listed in the ✎ column. Words sit beside the drawing, never on it.

## The three things the board film is built on

**1. The layout is a timeline.** The camera always moves forward. Each act is a row read left to
right, and each new row starts back on the left, one step lower:

| Row | Act | Period on the board |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Un système à part | the whole span, XVe → XIXe |
| 2 | Le besoin de bras | late 1400s, the first colonies |
| 3 | Le prix d'un être humain | XVIe – XVIIIe, the trade at its height |
| 4 | La traversée | the voyage |
| 5 | Ce que l'Afrique a perdu | abolition → today |
| 6 | Une idéologie | its own row, set apart: the ending |

There are three short pull-backs over the whole board, 2 s each: at the end of act 1, at the start
of act 4 (the turn) and before the last drawing.

**2. One motif: the dashed Atlantic route, in oxblood.** It is drawn by the pen on code-drawn maps
and inside drawings, and it changes in every act:
- **Act 1:** first drawn as a triangle between three continents (« trois continents »).
- **Act 2:** a single arrow from Europe turning south to Africa (« se sont tournés vers l'Afrique »).
- **Act 3:** splits in two directions: goods (armes, rhum) go in, people come out.
- **Act 4:** crosses the ocean, and one dash in five breaks off and never arrives (« 20 % »).
- **Act 5:** the route is cut at abolition, but the gun arrows stay inside Africa (« perdurent
  encore aujourd'hui »).
- **Act 6:** fades out on the map. The marks it left on the hands remain.

**3. The hook and the last drawing answer each other.**
- **G01 (hook):** two hands, wrists bound by a rope, in a tight close-up.
- **G44 (last drawing):** the same two hands, the rope gone, the marks still on the wrists
  (« bien après son abolition »).

They sit on the same column of the board, the first at the top and the last at the bottom.

**Historically accurate, violence implied.** Every drawing shows something that is documented:
- the real places: Elmina Castle, a Taíno village;
- the real objects: forked coffle yokes, branding irons, leg shackles, the *Brookes* plan;
- the real practices: canoes through the surf, the netting along the rail, « dancing the slaves »,
  captives refusing food.

No invented symbols. The moment of a wound is never drawn: we show the object or the scene just
before or after it, as the archive engravings of the period do. Each drawing's historical basis
goes in `SOURCES.md`, from sources that are not Wikipedia (the Slave Voyages database, Equiano's
*Interesting Narrative* of 1789, the *Brookes* plan of 1788, the UNESCO Slave Route project).

**No caricature.** The prompts ask for dignified, individual faces drawn with the same care for
everyone. No real person is depicted.

**Pen rhythm.** Key drawings (G01, G25 the ship's plan, G44) take 5–6 s of pen. Simple objects
take about 1 s. Everything else takes about 3 s. One drawing in three is locked off, with no push.

## The motif, in plain words

Across the film, the pen keeps drawing **the same dotted red line on the map of the Atlantic: the
path of the ships.** Each time it comes back, it shows the next step of the story:
- three continents joined (act 1);
- the ships turning toward Africa (act 2);
- guns going in and people going out (act 3);
- the crossing, where part of the line never arrives (act 4);
- the line cut at abolition while the guns stay (act 5).

It is a red thread for the viewer: one glance at it tells you where you are in the story. If you'd
rather do without it, the film works without it too.

## Narration facts to check (the voice is recorded, so tell me what you prefer)

1. **« Environ 20 % ne reverraient jamais la terre ferme »** (clip 7). The Slave Voyages database
   estimates about 12.5 million people embarked and 10.7 million landed: roughly **15 %** died on
   the crossing on average. The rate was higher in the early centuries and on some routes, so 20 %
   is the high end. My suggestion: keep the voice, but don't write the number on screen.
2. **« coupant les oreilles des esclaves comme preuve d'achat »** (clip 8). I could not tie this to
   a reliable historical source. Branding was the usual mark of ownership. Do you have the source
   for this line? If not, I'd suggest re-recording the sentence or cutting it.
3. **« des dizaines de millions de personnes »** (clip 12). The Atlantic trade carried away
   12.5 million. « Dizaines de millions » holds only if deaths during capture and the march to the
   coast are counted, and historians debate those figures (Patrick Manning). I'll write nothing on
   screen for it unless you want a number.
